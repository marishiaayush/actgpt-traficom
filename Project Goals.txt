Project Goals:
Saving Earth from Space Interferers Using Radio FrequenciesDevelop an analysis tool to protect national radio frequency use from planned space and terrestrial usage to prevent potential harmful interferences.

Problem Statement:
Using radio frequencies ('spectrum') requires preliminary planning and obeying International Telecommunications Union (ITU) rules ('International Radio Regulations'). The usage differs in different countries and regions. When using the spectrum in space (i.e. satellites) the challenge becomes global: Australian terrestrial usage does not interfere Finnish usage, but Australian satellite orbiting the Earth may cause interference to Finnish terrestrial usage in the same or adjacent frequency range. Due to this, ITU's Radio Bureau ('BR') International Frequency Information Circular ('IFIC') publications. The publications are available for space and terrestrial use separately, containing bi-monthly update on both pdf and database format.To analyse these, specialists need to identify items that may be relevant to Finland, compare them with other frequency information (especially the table of frequencies in Finland, but also the list of frequencies of special interest). When possible challenges are identified, the specialist assesses possible technical concerns and prepare follow-up correspondence to other administration planning the usage (for solving the situation (technically) before it happens, or to preserve rights to claim protection in case of realised harmful interference case).
The challenge is to build an AI-assisted workflow that can reduce the manual effort, enhance analysis coverage and increase the quality of analysis, while keeping the analysis transparent and technically reviewable. A first demonstrator may focus on the space-services side and should be designed so that the same architecture could later be extended to terrestrial frequency-planning workflows.A possible end-to-end workflow includes:ingesting relevant national information of frequency usage (regulations, additional frequency tables provided);detecting, retrieving and ingesting [new] ITU BR IFIC publications, especially API/A, CR/C and AP30 (including AP30A and AP30B);possible using ITU SpaceCom tool (or direct database logic) to identify interesting satellite systems;possible using ITU Gibc tool (or direct calculation logic) to calculate pfd values of the satellite systems;summarising the relevant content and frequency ranges affecting Finland or Finnish usage;cross-comparing the findings with information on national regulations ('M4', 'M15') and additional frequency tables;identifying items that may concern Finland based on both previous public material and possible synthetic data of frequency, pfd limits, and geographical locations;detecting and explaining potential concerns, including technical calculations on f. ex. power-flux density (pfd), where appropriate;identifying the relevant counterparty (administration) and drafting relevant correspondence template for expert review where possible interference concern is described.

Side quest: Terrestrial land-mobile noticesDemonstrate the architecture on terrestrial BR IFIC data limited to terrestrial land-mobile notices. The terrestrial side quest should show that the same workflow, with additions, modifications, or simplifications defined below, can support a different context without turning the submission into a second full challenge.Use consecutive terrestrial BR IFIC issues and identify what is new, modified or withdrawn;filter foreign assignments that may be relevant to Finland using notice data;determine the applicable ITU, CEPT/ECC and national regulatory framework;based on applicable regulatory framework, identify the applicable coordination or protection criterion such as ECC T/R 25-08;using the coordination or protection criteria, use deterministic method to calculate field strengths caused by the notified station. Use ITU-R P.1546 propagation model; andproduce expert reviewable findings showing the source notice, calculated field strengths, model parameters, assumptions and source citations.Additionally, surface a small number of trends or anomalies in neighboring countries administrations’ notices, such as new activity by band, geography, station class or bandwidth.

Guiding question:
How might we build an auditable AI-assisted workflow that automatically monitors ITU BR IFIC information, identifies cases relevant to Finland, supports technical (frequency and radio frequency power level) analysis, and prepares expert-reviewable draft correspondence?GSO and non-GSO satellite power flux densities are in special interest to recognize possible interfering usage, as well understanding trends and planned usage of new satellites systems which may interfere Finnish satellites already in use. Identifying these issues from huge mass of IFIC API/A, CR/C, and AP30 (AP30A and AP30B) information is essential, and there AI solution might be helpful.To successfully complete this task, you need to take into account available information on the Finnish radio frequency usage (i.e. Regulation 'M4' and additional frequency tables of special interest provided on the challenge platform) which are used on the comparisons and possible interference recognition. Additional data may be used, for example harmonised use of radio frequencies in Europe or other public radio frequency data usage sources, to raise further notes and concerns on the IFIC information.
